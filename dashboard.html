Market Structure

No single product has: camera + mic + speaker + near-eye display + USB-C data + wireless + continuous A/V streaming + deep programmatic control.

TierLinkExamplesLimitation
BLE DisplayBT LE, ~KB/sEven G1/G2, Vuzix Z100, Brilliant HaloCannot stream video
Wi-Fi CameraWi-Fi + SoCMentra Live, Rokid, INMO, OSAIGBattery 30-60 min; mostly no display
USB-C TetheredDP Alt Mode + UVCVuzix M400C, XREAL, RayNeo AirA cable; almost none have camera
Key insight: The tethered/external-compute architecture is the only one that currently gets you camera + display + high bandwidth + full control simultaneously. Over USB-C, interfaces are industry standards (UVC, UAC, DisplayPort) rather than a vendor's proprietary BLE protocol.

#1 — OSAIG / OpenSource-Ai-Glasses

github.com/Iam5tillLearning/OpenSource-Ai-Glasses
Rank #1 Apache-2.0 Root Linux 254★ 53↺
SiliconRockchip RV1106B (Cortex-A7)
CameraIMX219, 1080p
DisplayOptional 640×480 / 30° monocular
ConnectivityWi-Fi b/g/n, BT 5.3, USB 2.0
Battery180 mAh (~45 min)
Weight43 g
Updated2026-09-03 (active)
PurchaseTaobao / forwarder

The deepest developer access of anything you can buy. C/C++ SDK with GPIO events, zero-copy camera frames via shared memory, audio in/out, shared-memory framebuffer, BLE text channel, media arbitration, Unix-domain-socket IPC. RTSP out of the box (rtsp://<ip>:554/live/0). Root Linux. GDB + OpenOCD documented.

Published: system software, SDK, docs, 3D models, build system.
NOT published: schematics, PCB, gerbers, BOM.

Action: Pursue immediately. Buy two kits — they are cheap, and holding one collapses a month of speculation. This is the only purchasable root-Linux glasses platform with camera, audio, display, and streaming.

#2 — Vuzix (M400 / M400C / Z100 / Ultralite OEM)

Vuzix Corporation, Rochester NY (NASDAQ: VUZI)
Rank #2 Commercial OEM ADB (M400) UVC (M400C)
M400$1,800 · Android · IP67
M400C$1,299 · USB-C I/O only
Z100BLE display-only
OEMUltralite / Ultralite Pro via Quanta

The strongest OEM story of any Western vendor. M400C is the cleanest glasses-as-I/O device that exists: pure USB-C peripheral, DisplayPort Alt Mode display in, standard UVC camera out, 1080p60, powered by host. No on-glasses compute needed.

Ultralite Pro OEM = Snapdragon AR1 Gen 1 + dual Avegant LCoS + Vuzix binocular waveguides. Manufactured through Quanta Computer.

Action: Priority conversation #2. Ask about Ultralite OEM pricing/MOQ/NRE, whether M400C can be licensed/re-bodied, and whether waveguide + light engine can be bought at low volume.

Components

Displays & Optics

  • MICROOLED ActiveLook (Grenoble) — Complete near-eye HUD subsystem. Documented, open, free BLE interface. Apache-2.0 SDKs. Reference design with Quanta + STM. Shipping in ENGO, Julbo, Cosmo. If you want a monocular HUD you can drive without permission, this is it.
  • JBD (Jade Bird Display, Shanghai) — MicroLED dev kits: Hummingbird Mini I (mono) and Hummingbird I (poly). AmµLED projector: 0.35 cc / 0.6 g / 30° FOV. Roadrunner I/II for colour. Joint ref designs with Dispelix. Pricing [UNKNOWN].
  • Vuzix waveguides — Sold OEM standalone. US-made, defence-qualified (Collins Aerospace).
  • Avegant — LCoS light engines (in Ultralite Pro).
  • Lumus + Schott — Geometric reflective waveguide (in Meta Ray-Ban Display).
  • Sony micro-OLED — 0.44", 1920×1080 (in INMO Air3).

Compute

  • Rockchip RV1106/RV1106B — Tiny Linux camera SoC. Mature dev-board ecosystem (Luckfox Pico). Cheap, buyable, well documented. The reason OSAIG exists.
  • GAP9 (Greenwaves) — RISC-V ML MCU on open PULP platform. Core of ETH OpenGlass. Ultra-low power edge ML.
  • Alif Balletto — MCU+NPU. Used in Brilliant Halo.
  • Qualcomm AR1 Gen 1 / AR1+ — Industry-standard premium smart glasses SoC. Rokid, Samsung, Vuzix Ultralite Pro.
  • XIAO ESP32S3 Sense / omiGlass — $25 tier. Camera + mic on 3D-printed clip. BasedHardware, MIT. 13,387★ (omi). Different project from ETH OpenGlass.

Cross-Platform SDKs & Reverse Engineering

  • MentraOS — Apache-2.0, real, shipping, cross-vendor.
  • xg.glass SDK (HKUST SPARK) — One API across Rokid, Meta, Brilliant, RayNeo, Even, INMO, Omi + simulator. Honest about what works.
  • Extentos — Commercial phone-companion SDK. Public docs are the best-sourced openness comparison anywhere.
  • Even Realities G1/G2 — BLE protocol docs, pip package, Gadgetbridge support, firmware decompilation.
  • Rokid — buildwithfenna/rokid-docs (48★): YodaOS internals from firmware dumps.
Priority Conversations
  1. Mentra — Cayden Pierce / Alexander Israelov (San Francisco)
    The only org spanning the whole stack: Apache-2.0 OS, two shipped devices, an ODM relationship, and a stated business of licensing the core engine to manufacturers. Ask whether a camera+display device on their platform is possible, what the ODM relationship allows, and whether the K900 platform can be specified with a display.
  2. Vuzix OEM / Engineering Services (Rochester, NY)
    US-based, sells waveguides, sells reference designs, manufactures through Quanta, and already ships the one product that does glasses-as-pure-USB-C-I/O. Ask about Ultralite OEM Platform pricing, MOQ and NRE; whether the M400C USB-C viewer architecture can be licensed or re-bodied; whether the waveguide + light engine can be bought separately at low volume.
  3. Thundercomm (sales@thundercomm.com, Ali Mesri NA)
    The Snapdragon START turnkey path. Highest ceiling, most conventional, most expensive. Ask what the AR1+ AI glasses reference solution actually includes, and the real numbers on NRE, MOQ and timeline.
  4. ETH Zürich PBL — Julian Moosmann (system architecture) and Michele Magno (group lead)
    These are the people who can talk at the firmware → chip → PCB → camera → display → USB → sensor level. They publish board files, they've built glasses more than once, and a university lab is a far cheaper first technical conversation than a consultancy. Ask about licence terms on the OpenGlass hardware designs, whether the FPC-interposer main board could carry a display, and whether they take industry collaborations.
  5. OSAIG maintainer (iam5tilllearning@foxmail.com) — and SmartXY (vanda@topaiglasses.com)
    Two different bets on the Shenzhen supply chain. OSAIG is the cheapest way to have real hardware in hand with root access inside a month. SmartXY is a working ODM with its own factory (Dongguan, 5,000 m², 8 lines, 400k units/yr, OEM 1–3 months / ODM 6–9 months) whose K900 platform may be the hardware under Mentra Live.

Market Structure

No single product has: camera + mic + speaker + near-eye display + USB-C data + wireless + continuous A/V streaming + deep programmatic control.

Pass 2: Supply Chain Deep-Dive

Silicon Menu — The 6× Cost Lever

Mainboard + chip ≈ 45% of BOM
Architecture-defining Prices unverified

Three industry architectures the Chinese industry uses:

  1. Single SoC — AR1 (~$60?), W517 (~$10?). Most capable, most power and cost.
  2. MCU-SoC + external ISP — e.g. BES2800 + dedicated ISP. Near-AR1 image quality, BOM under ¥1000.
  3. SoC + MCU — AR1 for heavy work, BES2700/2800 for always-on low-power. Industry consensus: likely long-term winner.

Key SoCs:

  • Unisoc W517 — ~$10? 12nm, A75+A55, dual ISP, Android 11, 3D SiP. INMO Air2 uses it. INMO launched "AI glasses open platform" (影目 X series) targeting sub-¥1000.
  • Rockchip RV1106B — 0.5 TOPS INT8, 250mW@2MP video, 2mW standby. Under OSAIG.
  • Snapdragon AR1 Gen 1/+ — ~$60? Full Android. Ray-Ban, Rokid, Vuzix, Samsung.
  • BES2800/2900/6000 — Bestechnic MCU. In Even G1, MYVU, Xiaomi, Alibaba Quark. 2900 adds ISP; 6000 samples H1 2026.
  • SigmaStar SSC309QL — ISP companion for AI glasses. In Looktech.
  • Actions ATS3085 — MCU+DSP. Drives display + health + BT. In Halliday.
  • STM32N6 — 0.6 TOPS. In Tecno AI glasses.
  • GAP9 — ETH OpenGlass. 11.5h ML from 200mAh.
  • ESP32-S3 — $25 tier. omiGlass, LILYGO T-Glass, OSSG.
Key insight: Silicon choice is the single most expensive decision. W517 at ~$10 vs. AR1 at ~$60 is a 6× lever. BOM breakdown: mainboard+chip 45%, ROM+RAM 10%, sensors 9.47%, assembly 8.62%, power 5%, acoustics 3.16%.

Meta Smart Glasses — Privacy Backlash & Bans

August 2026: UK cinemas restricting Meta glasses over piracy. UK pubs, restaurants, theatres banning them. Germany nonprofit files criminal complaint. EU circling with potential regulation.

The tethered USB-C approach (M400C) also sidesteps this because the host controls when the camera is on.

EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA)

Reporting duties: September 2026 · Full compliance: December 2027
Active now Firmware = Hardware 10-year updates

The EU CRA treats software products the same as hardware for cybersecurity compliance. Applies to anything with digital elements sold in the EU.

  • No size threshold — one-person company = same obligations as a corporation
  • Self-assessment for most products (not Annex III) — no notified body, write documents once
  • Article 13(9): Every security update must stay available for 10 years after issue
  • Reporting never ends — vulnerability handling stops with support, reporting continues
  • Geographic: It doesn't matter where you live — matters that you sell to the EU

Smart glasses implication: If you ship firmware in the EU (OSAIG, MentraOS, custom builds), you need CE marking, technical file, 10-year update pipeline, and vulnerability reporting. Apache-2.0 does NOT exempt you if the software supports a commercial product.

Tethered architecture advantage: Glasses-as-I/O (M400C) with no on-glasses firmware means the compliance burden falls on the host device (already handled by Apple/Google/Samsung). On-glasses SoC architecture = you own the entire compliance stack.

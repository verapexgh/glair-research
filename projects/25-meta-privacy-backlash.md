## The Core Problem
Meta's Ray-Ban smart glasses (with camera) have triggered a **coordinated international backlash** over privacy, surveillance, and non-consensual recording. This is not a Meta-specific risk — it's an industry-wide one that affects any smart glasses with a visible or hidden camera.

## Implications for Smart Glasses Industry

### The Privacy Tension Is Real and Growing
This is not a fringe concern. It is:
- **National legislation** (Norway)
- **Criminal complaints** (Germany)
- **Industry bans** (UK cinemas, pubs, restaurants, theatres)
- **Mass consumer backlash** (185-point HN thread)

### Design Implications
| Approach | Privacy Risk | Examples |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **Camera + Display** | **Highest** — constant surveillance concern | Meta Ray-Ban, Mentra Live, Rokid |
| **Display only, no camera** | **Lowest** — no recording capability | Vuzix Z100, Mentra Mach1, RayNeo iO |
| **Camera only, no display** | **Medium** — recording but not covert | Mentra Live |
| **Tethered USB-C I/O** | **User-controlled** — host decides when camera on | Vuzix M400C |

### Strategic Takeaway for GLAIR
> **A camera-free or camera-indicated design is not optional — it may be required for market access in Europe within 1–2 years.**

The EU's existing GDPR, the new AI Act, the upcoming Cyber Resilience Act, and now venue-specific bans create a regulatory thicket for camera-equipped glasses.

### DuckDuckGo's Counter-Positioning
- **DuckDuckGo** launched "smart glasses" that are literally just sunglasses — no camera, no AI, no electronics, $35
- Marketing: "The world's most innovative anti-surveillance sunglasses"
- Tongue-in-cheek but **tapping into genuine consumer anxiety**
- Reference to Meta workers in Kenya viewing private Ray-Ban footage for AI training

## Recommendation
Any smart glasses project should:
1. **Assume privacy regulation is coming** — especially in EU and UK
2. **Design camera indication as mandatory** — LED, sound, or physical shutter
3. **Consider camera-free variants** for privacy-sensitive markets
4. **Document data handling** for GDPR/CRA compliance
5. **Watch Norway** — if they ban outright, other EU countries may follow
